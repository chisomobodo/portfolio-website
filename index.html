Arbiter: an AI agent that knows when to ask a human

Python · Agent orchestration · Claude tool use · Gmail · Azure SQL · Docker · Streamlit

An LLM-based agent connected to a real Gmail inbox. It reads unstructured order-change emails as they arrive and decides whether each request can be actioned, using live stock and dispatch data.

  • Orchestrates the whole workflow end to end: Gmail intake, LLM extraction and tool calls, ERP verification, an outbound queue with human approval, on-hold tracking while a customer replies, and a history of every outcome.
  • Rejects changes to orders that have already been dispatched and flags requests where stock is insufficient.
  • Sends ambiguous cases to a person rather than guessing, because a wrong automated decision costs far more than a short delay.
  • Works on live email, not pasted text: it receives customer emails from Gmail, sends clarification replies inside the original thread, and maps each reply back to the email and held order it belongs to, using layered matching.
  • Built as an application, not a notebook: role-based access control, an audit log of every automated and human decision, cloud deployment and a Streamlit dashboard. The orders and stock it checks against are synthetic.

Retail sales and profitability: which differences are real?

Python · pandas · SciPy · Matplotlib

Three stores, six products and 60 days of sales, merged with a pricing table into 1,080 validated records. Revenue, profit and margin are engineered so performance is judged on profit, not units sold.

  • One-way ANOVA: product identity drives demand, with a very large effect that price does not explain.
  • Welch's t-test: weekday and weekend profit are statistically indistinguishable.
  • Trend, 7-day rolling average and weekly seasonality show weekend demand is much less predictable, and that store totals are within 0.5% of each other.

High-value customer prediction: what the model can and cannot show

Python · scikit-learn · KNN imputation · Logistic Regression · pandas · SciPy

A binary classifier that flags high-value customers (spending score of 70 or more and income at or above the median) from age, income, spending score and gender, to support segmentation and targeted marketing.

  • Cleaned 200 customer records: standardised gender labels, removed invalid income entries and filled missing age and income with KNN imputation (k = 5).
  • Log-transformed the skewed income and winsorised four extreme outliers at the 1st and 99th percentiles, keeping every record.
  • Trained a class-weighted Logistic Regression on a stratified 80/20 split (12.5% high-value) and read the coefficients as odds ratios.

Loan approval prediction: choosing precision on purpose

Python · scikit-learn · Logistic Regression · GDPR · EU AI Act

Predicts loan approval from past applicants. The lender loses money when a bad applicant is approved, so the model was selected on precision, not accuracy.

  • Cleaned 13,731 records into 12,500 complete ones and engineered affordability features, disposable income and an outgoings-to-income ratio.
  • Compared Logistic Regression, Decision Tree and Random Forest with class weighting and 5-fold cross-validation. Logistic Regression had the highest cross-validated precision (0.702 vs 0.676 and 0.665).
  • Left out Postcode and Gender to avoid proxy discrimination, and checked the design against GDPR Article 22 and the EU AI Act.

Heart disease prediction: does tuning change what the model says?

Python · XGBoost · scikit-learn · SMOTE · SHAP · LIME

Compares four classifiers on 920 patient records from four UCI datasets, with imbalance handling and model explanations.

  • Stratified 5-fold cross-validation, with imputation, scaling and SMOTE applied inside the training folds only so nothing leaks into the test folds.
  • Tuned every model with GridSearchCV. Random Forest reached the highest mean ROC-AUC (0.886) and tuning lifted XGBoost from 0.859 to 0.883.
  • Used SHAP and LIME to explain predictions and to ask whether tuning changed how interpretable the models are.
